Painted panels hang along a wall, and each panel covers a closed span [left, right] of integer positions.

A laser fired straight at integer position x marks every panel whose span contains x, meaning left <=

x <= right. Spans may overlap, touch, be nested or repeat. Return the fewest shots needed so that

every panel is marked at least once. A shot may be fired at any integer position, including one

outside the listed spans.

A shot at 2 marks [0, 2] and [-3, 4]; a shot at 10 marks [3, 10] and

[9, 15]; [14, 16]

needs its own shot at 16. The spans [0, 2], [3, 10] and [14, 16] are pairwise disjoint,

so three shots are unavoidable.

The spans are pairwise disjoint, so no position lies

in two of them and each needs its own shot.

Firing at the smallest right end among unmarked panels marks the most panels any choice for that panel

could.

Copy and sort the panels by right end. Walk them, keeping the last shot position. A panel with

left > lastShot is unmarked: count a shot and fire at its right end.

Take the panel with the smallest right end r. Some shot must hit it, and every other panel

ends at or after r, so a panel that contains any shot inside this one also contains r.

Firing at r is therefore never worse, so an exchange argument shows the greedy choice is safe. After

firing, all panels with left <= r are marked; the rest are handled the same way. Sorting by

right end is O(n log n), the scan is O(n), and the sorted copy costs O(n) memory (O(1)

if sorting the input in place is allowed).
