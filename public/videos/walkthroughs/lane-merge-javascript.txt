A swim meet records finishing times per lane, and each lane's list is already sorted from fastest to

slowest (smallest to largest). Merge all lanes into one list of every time, sorted from smallest to largest.

Some lanes may be empty.

All eight

times in order.

No lanes, no times.

Only the k lane fronts are candidates for the next output, and a heap finds the smallest in

O(log k).

Push [value, lane, index] for each non-empty lane. Repeatedly pop the minimum, output its value, and push the

next element of that lane if any.

The next output is always one of the k lane fronts, so I keep just those in a

min-heap. Each pop outputs a value

and pushes its lane's successor, so scanning the k lanes and processing N values costs O(k + N

log(k + 1)) total with O(k)

heap space. The initial scan still costs O(k) when all lanes are empty. Merging lanes pairwise in rounds

gives the same bound without a heap.

The heap starts with fronts 1 (lane 0), 1 (lane 1) and 2 (lane 2).

Pop 1 (lane 0) and push 4. Pop 1 (lane 1) and push 3. Pop 2 and push

6. Pop 3 and push 4…

Output: [1,1,2,3,4,4,5,6].
