People wait in a single line at a ticket window. The array tickets holds how many tickets each

person still needs, front of the line first. Every second, the person at the front buys exactly one

ticket. If they still need more, they walk to the back of the line; otherwise they leave. Return

the second at which the person who started at zero-based index position buys their last ticket.

Seconds 1–3 serve persons 0, 1, 2 once each. Person 2 leaves. Second 4 serves person 0, who

leaves. Second 5 serves person 1, and second 6 serves person 1 again for their last ticket.

Person 0 buys at seconds 1, 3, 4 and 5; person 1 leaves after second 2.

The target buys one ticket per round, so the answer is the number of purchases that happen before

or during the target's final purchase, and every other person makes a predictable number of those.

Let needed be the target's count. A person at or before the target is served min(tickets, needed) times.

A person after the target is served min(tickets, needed - 1) times because the target finishes earlier in

the last round. Sum those contributions.

Rather than simulate, I count purchases. Everyone in front of the target, and the target, is served once

per round for up to needed rounds; everyone behind gets one round fewer because the target leaves before

them in the final round. Summing min values gives the answer in one linear pass with no queue.

For [2, 3, 1] and position 1, needed is 3.

Person 0 contributes min(2, 3) = 2, person 1 contributes min(3, 3) = 3, person 2 contributes min(1,

2) = 1.

The total is 6, matching the simulation.

O(n). O(1)
