A delivery cart crosses a toll grid from the top-left cell to the bottom-right cell. grid[r][c] is the

toll for being in cell (r, c): the cart pays the toll of its starting cell and of

every cell it moves into. A normal move goes one cell right or one cell down. Once per

trip, instead of a normal move, the cart may take the service slide: it glides any number of

cells (at least one) to the right along its current row, paying nothing for any cell it passes

or lands on. Return the smallest total toll for the trip. The slide is optional.

Pay 4 for the start, slide along the top row to the last column for free, then move

down onto 1 and 7. The total is

4 + 1 + 7 = 12. Without the slide, the cheapest right/down trip costs 27 (4 +

7 + 2 + 6 + 1 + 7).

The cart starts on the goal and pays the toll of that one cell.

The best place to start a slide that lands in column c is the cheapest unused arrival anywhere

to its left in the same row, and that minimum can be carried along as the row is

swept.

Sweep each row left to right with rolling arrays for plain and slid. At each column compute plain

from above/left, compute slid as the smaller of (slid from above/left + toll) and the cheapest plain value

seen earlier in this row, then update that running minimum with the new plain value.

Two states per cell turn an exponential search into 2 * rows * cols values. The only non-local

transition is the slide, and since it

always starts from an earlier cell of the same row, a running minimum answers it in O(1). Rolling

the rows keeps memory at one row.

plain along row 0 is 4, 11, 13, 22; the running minimum before column 3 is 4, so

slid at column 3 is 4.

Moving down from slid at (1, 3) = 5 gives 5 + 7 = 12 at (2, 3),

which beats plain there (27), so the answer is 12.

O(rows * cols). O(cols)
