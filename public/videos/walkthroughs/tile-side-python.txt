A carpenter wants to cut every plank in planks into equal pieces of one shared whole-number length, with

no leftover wood on any plank. Return the longest piece length that works for every plank.

Pieces of length 6 divide all three planks, and nothing longer does.

Only length 1 divides both 7 and 9.

The answer is the greatest common divisor of all lengths, and gcd can be combined one plank at

a time.

Define gcd(a, b) by repeatedly replacing (a, b) with (b, a % b). Reduce the planks with it.

A piece length must divide every plank, so the longest one is the gcd of the whole array.

gcd is associative, so I fold it across the planks with Euclid's algorithm, which takes logarithmic steps per

pair. That's O(n log M) time and constant space. I could also stop early once the running gcd

hits 1.

gcd(12, 18): (12, 18) → (18, 12) → (12, 6) → (6, 0), giving 6.

gcd(6, 30): (6, 30) → (30, 6) → (6, 0), giving 6.

O(n log M) for maximum length M. O(1)
