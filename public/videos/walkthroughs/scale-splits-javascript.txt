A balance scale has a left pan and a right pan. Every crate in weights must go on

exactly one of the two pans. Crates are distinct even when their weights match, and swapping every crate

to the other pan gives a different placement. Count the placements for which the two pan totals differ

by at most tolerance. The count can reach 2^36, which a JavaScript number holds exactly.

The pans balance exactly only when 3, 5 and 8 sit on one pan against the 16, since

3 + 5 + 8 = 16. That placement and its mirror image are the 2 answers.

There are 2^6 = 64 placements. The total weight is 33, so the pan totals always differ by

an odd number, and exactly 12 of the 64 placements differ by 1 or 3.

The difference splits into a first-half part and a second-half part, so count pairs of half-sums instead of

whole placements.

For each half, build the sorted list of all signed sums by starting from [0] and merging (sums

+ w) with (sums - w) for each crate. Then count pairs with x + y <= tolerance

and pairs with x + y <= -tolerance - 1 using a two-pointer pass (ascending x, descending pointer

into y) and subtract.

Each placement's difference is a half-sum from the first 18 crates plus a half-sum from the last 18,

and the two halves are independent. That replaces 2^36 placements by two lists of 2^18 sums and a

counting problem on

two sorted arrays, solved by a monotone pointer. Building the lists by merging x + w and x

- w keeps them sorted without ever calling sort. The count is exact in a double because it

never exceeds 2^36.

For [3, 5, 8, 16] the first half [3, 5] gives sums -8, -2, 2, 8 and the

second half [8, 16] gives -24, -8, 8, 24.

Only -8 + 8 and 8 + -8 are zero, so atMost(0) - atMost(-1) = 2.

O(2^(n/2)): about 2 * 262,144 sums, built by merging, and two linear two-pointer sweeps at n = 36.

O(2^(n/2)) doubles
