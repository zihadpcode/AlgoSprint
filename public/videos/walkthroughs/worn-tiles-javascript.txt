A hallway has length floor tiles, numbered 0 to length - 1. A cleaning pass [from, to] scrubs

every tile from from to to, both ends included. A tile wears out once at least limit passes

have scrubbed it. Passes may overlap, repeat or touch each other in any order. Return how many tiles

are worn out after all the passes.

Tiles 4, 5 and 6 are covered by two or more passes (tile 5 by three), and tiles

8 and 9

are covered by the second and fourth passes. That makes 5 worn tiles. Every other tile is scrubbed

at most once.

One pass scrubs every tile exactly once, so no tile reaches the limit of 2.

Record only where each pass starts and stops; the running sum of those marks is the pass count

of every tile.

Allocate change of size length + 1. For each pass add 1 at from and subtract 1 at

to + 1. Sweep the tiles left to right keeping a running total of change and count the

tiles where it is at least limit.

A pass is an interval, and the number of passes covering a tile is the sum of +1

marks at or before it minus -1 marks before it. A difference array stores those marks, and one

prefix-sum sweep rebuilds every tile's count. That turns 350 million paint steps into about 103,500 operations.

For the first example the marks are +1 at tiles 2, 4, 5 and 8 and -1 at

tiles 7, 10, 6 and 12.

The running sum reaches 2 at tile 4, 3 at tile 5, 2 at tile 6, drops at

tile 7, is 2 again at tiles 8 and 9, so 5 tiles meet the limit.

O(length + passes). O(length)
