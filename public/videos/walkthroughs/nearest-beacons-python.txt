A rescue drone at the origin (0, 0) can check k distress beacons before its battery runs low.

Each beacon is [x, y]. Return the k beacons closest to the origin by straight-line distance, ordered from

closest to farthest. Break distance ties by smaller x, then by smaller y. Beacons may share a position;

each one counts separately.

Squared distances are 18, 26 and

20, so [3, 3] then [-2, 4].

All are at distance 1;

ties go by x, then y:

[-1, 0], [0, -1], [0, 1].

Only the k best candidates matter; a max-heap exposes the worst of them for quick replacement.

Use heapq with negated (squared distance, x, y) tuples so the minimum tuple represents the worst selected

beacon. Fill the heap to k, then replace its root only when a new negated tuple is larger.

Convert the survivors back to coordinates and sort by the positive distance-and-coordinate key.

Maintain the k best beacons in a max-heap ordered by (distance², x, y). Since heapq is a min-heap,

negate every component of that tuple; its root then represents the worst selected beacon. New better beacons

replace the root. Selection costs O(n log k), final ordering costs O(k log k), and the heap uses

O(k) space.

k = 2. Push [3,3] (18) and [5,-1] (26); the root is [5,-1].

[-2,4] (20) beats 26, so it replaces the root, and the heap is {[3,3], [-2,4]}.

Sorted: [3,3], [-2,4].

O(n log k + k log k). O(k)
