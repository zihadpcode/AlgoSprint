A flat roof has a row of parapet blocks whose heights are listed in the array heights, one

unit wide each. After heavy rain, water settles in the dips between taller blocks; water above a block

rises to the lower of the tallest block on its left and the tallest block on its right,

and never spills off either end. Return the total volume of water held, in square units. Fewer than

three blocks hold no water.

The blocks of height 2 and 3 bound the middle: water levels are 2, 1 and 2 above

the three middle blocks, for 5 units.

The heights only descend, so nothing is held.

When the block at the left pointer is lower than the block at the right pointer, the left

block's water level is decided by the left wall alone, because some block at least as tall as

the right block exists on its right.

Keep pointers at both ends and the tallest block seen from each side. Repeatedly process the side whose

current block is lower: update that side's wall, add the water above the block, and move the pointer

inward.

I move two pointers inward and always settle the lower side. If the left block is lower than

the right block, the right side already guarantees a wall at least as tall as the

right block, so the water over the left block is exactly leftWall minus its height. Each step moves

one pointer, so the pass is linear with constant memory, matching the prefix-array solution without the arrays.

For [2, 0, 1, 0, 3], the left block 2 is lower than the right block 3, so

the left side is settled: leftWall = 2, no water, left moves to index 1.

Index 1 (0) adds 2; index 2 (1) adds 1; index 3 (0) adds 2, each compared against

the right block 3.

The pointers meet and the total is 5.

O(n). O(1)
