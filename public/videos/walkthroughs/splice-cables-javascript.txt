A crew must join several cable pieces into one continuous cable. Joining two pieces of lengths a and

b costs a + b units of labor and produces one piece of length a + b. Given

the piece lengths in the array lengths, return the minimum total labor needed to join every piece into

a single cable. A single piece or no pieces needs no labor.

Join 2 and 3 for 5, then 4 and 5 for 9, then 6 and 9 for 15.

The total is 5 + 9 + 15 = 29, and no other order is cheaper.

One piece is already a single cable.

The greedy rule only ever needs the two smallest pieces, which is exactly what a min-heap serves efficiently.

Push all lengths into a min-heap. While at least two pieces remain, pop the two smallest, add their

sum to the labor, and push the sum back.

Each join should combine the two shortest available pieces, because a piece's length is paid again in every

later join that includes it. A binary min-heap gives the two smallest in O(log n) each, so n

−

1 joins cost O(n log n). I implement the heap by hand since JavaScript has no built-in priority

queue.

For [4, 3, 2, 6], the heap yields 2 and 3; labor becomes 5 and 5 is pushed

back.

Next it yields 4 and 5; labor becomes 14 and 9 is pushed.

Finally 6 and 9 are joined for 15, so the labor totals 29.
