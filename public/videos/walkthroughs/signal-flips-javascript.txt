Two relay switches each store a non-negative integer as 31 binary toggles. To turn the pattern of a

into the pattern of b, a technician flips toggles one at a time. Return how many toggles must

be flipped, which is the number of bit positions where a and b differ.

1 is 001 and 4 is 100; they differ in two positions.

Identical patterns need no flips.

XOR marks every differing position with a 1 bit.

Compute x = a ^ b and repeatedly clear its lowest set bit with x &= x -

1, counting iterations.

XOR leaves a 1 wherever the bits differ, so the answer is the popcount of a ^ b.

Clearing the lowest set bit with x & (x - 1) runs once per set bit. Many languages

also expose popcount as a built-in.

1 ^ 4 = 5 (101).

5 & 4 = 4, count 1; 4 & 3 = 0, count 2.

O(number of differing bits). O(1)
