A row of adjacent buildings, each one unit wide, has the heights listed in heights. An advertiser wants

to paint one axis-aligned rectangular billboard across the fronts of consecutive buildings, from ground level

up. The billboard can be no taller than the shortest building it covers. Return the largest possible billboard

area.

Covering the buildings of height 5 and 6 at height 5 gives area 10.

The single building of height 4 beats 2 × 2.

Each building's best billboard spans until the nearest shorter building on each side, and an increasing stack

discovers both boundaries when a shorter building arrives.

Scan i from 0 to n, using height 0 at i = n. While the stack's top height

is >= the current height, pop it: its right boundary is i and its left boundary is the

new stack top (or -1). Update the best area. Push i.

For each building I want the widest stretch where it's the minimum, which ends at the nearest shorter

building on each side. I keep indices in increasing height order. When a shorter height arrives, each popped

building has found its right boundary at i, and the element beneath it is its left boundary. A

zero sentinel flushes the stack. Every index is pushed and popped once, O(n). Popping on >= handles equal

heights, because the last equal building popped computes the full width.

Push 0 (2). Height 1 pops 2: width 1, area 2. Push 1, 2 (5), 3 (6).

Height 2 pops 6 (width 1, area 6) and 5 (width 2, area 10). Push 4 (2), 5

(3).

The sentinel pops 3 (area 3), 2 (width 4, area 8) and 1 (width 6, area 6). The

best is 10.

O(n). O(n)
