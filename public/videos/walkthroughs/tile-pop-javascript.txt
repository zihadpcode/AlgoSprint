A rail holds a row of tiles whose weights are given in weights. Two tiles that are currently

next to each other can be popped together when their weights differ by at most tolerance. After a

pop the tiles on either side close the gap and become neighbours. You may pop as many pairs

as you like, in any order, or stop at any moment. Your score is the total weight of

all tiles that were popped. Return the maximum score.

Pop 9 and 8 (they differ by 1). Then 6 and 5 are neighbours, so pop them. Now

4 and 2 are neighbours and differ by 2, so pop them too. Every tile is popped, for

a score of 4 + 6 + 5 + 9 + 8 + 2 = 34.

No two neighbouring tiles differ by 5 or less, so nothing can be popped and the score is

0.

Popped tiles form non-crossing pairs, and everything inside a pair is popped first, so a stretch's answer is

built from smaller stretches.

Let clear[i][j] say stretch i..j can be fully popped and best[i][j] be its best score. Both come from

the leftmost tile i: either best skips it (best[i+1][j]), or i pairs with a k (k - i

odd) with |w[i] - w[k]| <= tolerance and clear[i+1][k-1], giving sum(i..k) + best[k+1][j]; clear[i][j] holds if

such a k also has clear[k+1][j]. Empty stretches are clear with score 0.

A popped pair (i, k) requires the tiles between them to be gone first, and they must pair

among themselves, so pairs nest or sit side by side but never cross. That is a classic interval

DP: the state is a

stretch, and the transition chooses the partner of its leftmost tile. Two tables are needed because 'can this

stretch be emptied' is a different question from 'how much can I score in it'. The cubic cost

comes from the choice of k.

For the first example, tiles 9 and 8 (indices 3, 4) are clear with best 17, and 6

and 5 (indices 1, 2) are clear with best 11.

The tiles of weight 4 and 2 (indices 0 and 5) differ by 2 and enclose the cleared

stretch of indices 1 to 4, so clear[0][5] is true and best[0][5] = 34.

O(n^3): roughly n^3 / 12, about 670,000 inner steps at n = 200. O(n^2)
