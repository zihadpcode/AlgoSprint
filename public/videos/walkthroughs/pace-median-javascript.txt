A running club records finishing paces one at a time in paces. After each new pace arrives, the

club board shows the median of all paces recorded so far: the middle value when the count is

odd, or the average of the two middle values when it is even (which may end in .5).

Return the list of medians shown after each arrival.

Medians: 5, then (5 + 15) / 2 = 10, then 5, then (3 + 5) / 2

= 4.

Equal paces: 2, then 2.

The median sits between the lower half's maximum and the upper half's minimum, and heaps expose both in

O(1).

lower is a max-heap (a min-heap of negated values), upper a min-heap. Insert into lower when the pace

is <= lower's top, else into upper. If lower has two more than upper, move its top to

upper; if upper is larger, move its top to lower. Report the median from the tops.

I keep the lower half in a max-heap and the upper half in a min-heap, with the lower

half equal in size or one

larger. Each arrival goes to the side its value belongs on, then at most one element moves across

to rebalance. The median comes straight from

the tops. That's O(log n) per arrival. JavaScript has no built-in heap, so I wrote a small min-heap

and store negated values for the max-heap.

5 → lower {5}: median 5. 15 → upper {15}: median 10.

1 → lower {5,1}, sizes 2/1: median 5.

3 → lower {5,3,1} is too big, so move 5 to upper {5,15}. Median (3 + 5) /

2 = 4.

O(n log n). O(n)
