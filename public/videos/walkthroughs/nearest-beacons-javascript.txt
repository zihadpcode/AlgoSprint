A rescue drone at the origin (0, 0) can check k distress beacons before its battery runs low.

Each beacon is [x, y]. Return the k beacons closest to the origin by straight-line distance, ordered from

closest to farthest. Break distance ties by smaller x, then by smaller y. Beacons may share a position;

each one counts separately.

Squared distances are 18, 26 and

20, so [3, 3] then [-2, 4].

All are at distance 1;

ties go by x, then y:

[-1, 0], [0, -1], [0, 1].

Only the k best candidates matter; a max-heap exposes the worst of them for quick replacement.

Use a comparator on (squared distance, x, y). Push beacons until the heap has k; afterwards, if a

beacon is better than the heap's top (its worst), replace the top and sift down. Finally sort the

k survivors with the comparator.

I keep the k best beacons in a max-heap keyed by (distance², x, y), so the root is

the worst of the current best. Each new beacon either loses to the root

or replaces it and sifts down. That's O(n log k) with O(k) memory, then I sort the k

survivors. Quickselect would give average O(n), but the heap also works on a stream.

k = 2. Push [3,3] (18) and [5,-1] (26); the root is [5,-1].

[-2,4] (20) beats 26, so it replaces the root, and the heap is {[3,3], [-2,4]}.

Sorted: [3,3], [-2,4].

O(n log k + k log k). O(k)
