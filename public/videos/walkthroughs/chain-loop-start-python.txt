A conveyor system is modeled as a linked chain of stations. Station i forwards items to station next[i],

or to nowhere when next[i] is -1. Items enter at station head, or the chain is empty when

head is -1. Because of a wiring mistake, the chain may eventually loop back to an earlier station

forever. Return the index of the first station that is visited twice when following the chain from head,

which is where the loop begins. Return -1 when the chain ends. Aim for constant extra memory.

Items travel 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 1, so station 1 is the loop start.

The chain ends at station 2 and never loops.

A pointer moving two steps at a time gains one station per move on a pointer moving one

step, so inside a loop they must meet; the meeting point is positioned so that a walk from

the head and a walk from the meeting point reach the loop start together.

Phase one advances slow by one and fast by two until they coincide or fast reaches the end.

Phase two restarts one pointer at the head and moves both one step at a time; the station

where they meet is the loop start.

I run a slow and a fast pointer; if the fast pointer hits the end there is no

loop, otherwise they meet inside it. If the tail before the loop has length a and the loop

has length c, the meeting point sits a steps before the loop start when measured around the loop,

so a second walk from the head and from the meeting point converges exactly at the loop start.

Two passes, constant memory.

For next = [1, 2, 3, 1] and head 0: slow goes 1, 2, 3 while fast goes

2, 1, 3, meeting at station 3.

Restart entry at 0: entry moves to 1 while slow moves from 3 to 1. They coincide at

station 1.

Station 1 is the loop start, matching the direct walk.

O(n). O(1)
