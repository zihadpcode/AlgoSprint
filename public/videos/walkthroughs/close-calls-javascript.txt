A weather office logged one integer temperature reading per station. Two stations make a close call when their

readings differ by at most tolerance. Count the pairs of positions i < j that make a close

call. Stations with equal readings are still separate stations, so equal readings form a pair too.

The close calls are the two 3s, 9 with 11, 11 with 12, and 12 with 14. Pairs

such as 9 and 12 differ by 3 and do not count, so the answer is 4.

All three readings are equal, so each of the 3 position pairs is a close call even with

tolerance 0.

In sorted order, the readings within tolerance below a given reading are one contiguous block ending right

before it.

Sort a copy of the readings. For each right index, advance left while sorted[right] - sorted[left] exceeds

tolerance. Every index from left to right - 1 pairs with right, so add right - left.

Sorting turns the pair condition into a range condition: for a fixed reading, partners within tolerance are an

unbroken stretch just below it. Because readings only increase along the sweep, the left boundary moves only

forward, so the sweep is linear after the O(n log n) sort. A typed array sort compares numerically.

Because every reading is at most 999, a counting array plus prefix sums over the 1,000 possible values

would also work in O(n + 1000); the sorted two-pointer sweep is the version that does not rely

on that small range.

The example becomes [3, 3, 9, 11, 12, 14, 20].

The second 3 adds 1, 9 adds 0, 11 adds 1, 12 adds 1 (11 only), 14 adds

1 (12 only) and 20 adds 0, giving 4.

O(n log n) for the sort plus O(n) for the sweep. O(n) for the sorted copy
