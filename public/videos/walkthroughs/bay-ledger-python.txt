A loading dock has bays numbered 1 to bays in a row, all empty at first. Process the

operations in order. ["set", bay, count] makes that bay hold exactly count boxes (replacing whatever it held).

["total", bay] reports how many boxes are in bays 1 to bay together. ["locate", k] numbers the boxes

1, 2, 3, ... starting from bay 1 and moving outward, and reports the bay that holds box

number k, which is the smallest b such that bays 1 to b hold at least k boxes

together; it reports -1 if the whole dock holds fewer than k boxes. Return the reports of the

total and locate operations in order. A set operation produces no report.

After the first two sets bay 3 holds 5 boxes and bay 6 holds 2. Bays 1 to

4 hold 5 boxes. Box 6 is the first box of bay 6

(bays 1 to 3 hold only 5), so locate 6 reports 6. Then bay 3 drops to 1

and bay 8 gets 9 boxes, so the running totals are 1 at

bay 3, 3 at bay 6 and 12 at bay 8. Box 6 is now in bay 8,

and box 14 does not exist, so the reports are 5, 6, 8, -1.

The dock is empty for the first two locates,

so both report -1. After setting 3 boxes, total

reports 3 and box 3 lives in bay 1.

A Fenwick tree makes both point changes and prefix totals logarithmic, and its layout lets a single descent

find the first bay reaching a target.

Keep counts in held and partial sums in tree. set adds (new - old) up the update chain.

total adds down the query chain. locate starts at position 0, tries jumps of the largest power of

two down to 1, takes a jump when the stored sum is below the remaining target (subtracting it),

and finally answers position + 1 if that bay exists, else -1.

The Fenwick tree stores in cell i the sum of the last i & -i bays, so every

prefix is a sum of at most 17 cells. Because totals are monotone in the bay number, the

first

bay reaching k can be found by building the largest prefix still below k one power of two

at a time; the answer is the bay after it. Everything is logarithmic and the memory is one

array.

set 3 = 5 adds 5 to cells 3 and 4 and 8; set 6 = 2 adds

2 to cells 6 and 8.

For locate 6 the jump of 8 has stored sum 7, which is not below 6, so it

is rejected. The jump of 4 has sum 5, below 6, so it is taken and the remaining

target becomes 1. The jump of 2 reaches cell 6 with sum 2, not below 1, so it

is rejected; the jump of 1 reaches cell 5 with sum 0, below 1, so it is taken.

The position is 5 and the answer is bay 6.

O(bays + operations * log bays): about 17 steps per operation. O(bays)
