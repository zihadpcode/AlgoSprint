A train is a linked chain of cars: car i is followed by car next[i], or by nothing

when next[i] is -1, and head is the first car. The chain visits every car exactly once. Uncouple

the k-th car counting from the back (k = 1 is the last car) and reconnect the train.

Return the car indices from front to back after the removal.

The second car from the back

is 3, leaving [0, 1, 2, 4].

Removing the only car leaves an empty train.

A gap of k cars between two pointers means that when the lead reaches the end, the trail

is right before the target.

Copy the links. Advance lead k times from head. If lead is -1, the head is removed. Otherwise

move lead and trail together until lead is the last car, then bypass trail's next car. Read off

the new order.

Two pointers k apart find the predecessor of the k-th car from the back in one pass. Removing

the head is the edge case; with node objects a dummy node in front of the head removes

that special case.

k = 2: lead moves 0→1→2.

Advance together until lead is 4: trail ends at 2.

Bypass 3: 2→4. Order [0,1,2,4].

O(n). O(1) beyond the copied links and output
